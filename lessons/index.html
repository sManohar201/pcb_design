PCB Layout Fundamentals

Learn why boards are laid out the way they are, using simulations where you move parts, cut planes and reroute traces, then see the consequence. The running theme is the kind of board you want to build: a carrier board for a Jetson-class module, with switching regulators, USB/PCIe/CSI and sensitive analog circuits on the same copper.

1 · Read the ideaEach section starts with a short explanation tied to your PDFs (Jones, ROHM, Sierra).
2 · Break it in the labDrag, slide and toggle. Meters and the What happens panel update live with the consequence.
3 · Take the rule awayEvery lab ends with a rule of thumb, the reason behind it, and how to apply it in Altium.
About the modelsThe simulations use real formulas (IPC-2221, microstrip, via-loop inductance, Laplace field solves, Ott's loop radiation). They are simplified, so trust the trends and orders of magnitude, not the third digit.
Reference documents in your folder

PCB_Layout_Tutorial_e.pdf: David L. Jones, PCB Design Tutorial (2004). Basics, placement, DFM. Some grounding advice is dated.
TWHB-03e.pdf: ROHM, DC/DC Buck Converter PCB Layout Basics. Hot loops, input capacitors, feedback, AGND/PGND.
High-Speed PCB Design Guide.pdf: Sierra Circuits (2020). Signal integrity, stackups, PDN target impedance, vias.

For Jetson-specific numbers (impedances, length limits, via budgets per interface), use NVIDIA's Product Design Guide for your module alongside this course.
