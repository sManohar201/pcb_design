Lesson 9 · High-speed signals

Crosstalk

Lesson 1.3 showed one trace disturbing its neighbour through mutual inductance and capacitance. On a transmission line the effect has a direction and a shape: near-end crosstalk travels back toward the driver, far-end crosstalk rides along with the edge. Their behaviour leads directly to the spacing rules in your PDFs.

9.1 Near-end and far-end crosstalk

As the aggressor's edge travels down its line, it couples a little energy into the victim at every point. Part of it heads backward to the victim's near end (NEXT). It arrives spread out over the whole round trip, so once the coupled length is longer than about half the edge length, it stops growing and saturates. Part of it travels forward with the aggressor's edge (FEXT) and piles up the whole way, so it grows with length. The inductive and capacitive parts of FEXT have opposite signs. In a stripline they cancel almost exactly; in a microstrip they don't, because some field is in air.

Rule of thumb

Use length to control FEXT (keep parallel runs short, or route on inner stripline layers). Use spacing and dielectric height to control NEXT: once you're past the saturation length, only D/H reduces it. Sierra §2.3.3: increase spacing, keep traces close to the plane, use differential signalling, route adjacent layers orthogonally, and reduce parallel run length.

9.2 Spacing rules: 3W, guard traces and adjacent layers

The "3W rule" says traces should be at least three trace widths apart, centre to centre. It is a shortcut for "spacing large compared with height above the plane", since W is usually about 1–2× H for a 50 Ω line. Below, a victim sits in the middle of a 5-trace bus. Rule of thumb

Default to 3W centre-to-centre for ordinary signals, and more (5W, or 30–50 mil) around clocks and fast pairs (Sierra §3.5.3). A guard trace only helps if it is grounded with stitching vias along its length. An unconnected or single-ended guard can make crosstalk worse. Never run two adjacent signal layers in parallel; route them orthogonally, or better, put a plane between them (lesson 2).

In Altium

Use Clearance rules by net class (e.g. CLOCKS 0.5 mm to everything). For parallel-run limits, the Parallel Segment rule (Design » Rules » High Speed) caps how long two nets may run side by side at a given gap. It is the direct way to control FEXT.
