★ Review a bad board

Jones' last step before manufacture: "Get someone to check it." This time that someone is you. Below is a 4-layer carrier board for a module, with a buck converter, USB 3, M.2, Ethernet, HDMI, a camera connector and audio. It contains 13 layout mistakes, each one from an earlier lesson. Switch layers, click wherever you see a problem, and use the fix button to see what it should look like.

How to play

  • Pick a layer view. Some problems only show on the GND or power plane, or on the bottom layer. Plane views also show, faintly, the signals that use that plane as their reference
  • Click the spot you think is wrong. Hits are numbered; misses are counted
  • Each find explains the consequence and links back to its lesson
  • Stuck? Hint points to an area and a layer

The whole course in five lines

1. Every signal is a loop; give its return current an unbroken path right next to it (lessons 1, 3). 2. One solid ground, with placement doing the separating (4). 3. Put the switching current loops and the decoupling where the current is; keep them tiny (5, 6). 4. Fast signals are transmission lines: control impedance, terminate, match in time, count vias (7–11). 5. Placement decides all of the above, and manufacturing decides what it costs (12, 13).
