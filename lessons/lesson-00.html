Board tour: anatomy of a carrier board

A compute module (such as a Jetson Orin Nano or NX) carries the processor, memory and power management on a small board with one large connector. The carrier board you design takes power in, makes the rails the module and peripherals need, and brings each interface out to a connector. Before learning the rules, take a tour: what is on such a board, where it goes, and which lesson explains it.

0.2 Which nets are "high-speed"?

A net needs transmission-line care (controlled impedance, termination, an unbroken return path, few vias) when its trace is long compared with the length of its edge in copper. An edge rising in 50 ps occupies only about 7 mm of trace, so a 30 mm USB route holds several edge lengths. An I²C edge rising in 300 ns stretches over 40 m, so no trace on a board is long for it. Edge time matters, not clock frequency: a 25 MHz clock from a modern driver can have 1 ns edges. Pick an interface:

Rule of thumb

Edge length in copper ≈ rise time ÷ 6.5 ps/mm. A route longer than about one sixth of the edge length behaves as a transmission line. Below that it is a lumped wire, and only its resistance and inductance matter (lesson 1). Power nets are never about reflections; they are about resistance, inductance and loops.
